Beyond Token Count

Three ways models spend inference compute — and why they are not the same.

A technical review by Prateek Dutta that separates parallel sampling, tree search, and self-consistency into independent axes of test-time compute.

Live sketch of the three-axis framework

Paper context

From training laws to test-time compute

For years, progress in language models was framed by training-compute scaling. The rise of reasoning models such as OpenAI’s o-series and DeepSeek-R1 shifted attention to inference-time compute: accuracy that improves when a model is allowed to “think longer” at answer time.

The literature often collapses that idea into a single “more test-time compute” curve. This review argues that the gain can come from different mechanisms — more samples, deeper search, or voting over answers — each with its own cost–accuracy tradeoff, failure mode, and task affinity.

  • Contribution. A three-axis taxonomy of inference compute, not a new empirical benchmark.
  • Scope. Critical synthesis of roughly forty studies (2023–2026), with a comparison table and open-problems agenda.
  • Takeaway. Scaling behavior differs meaningfully across axes and task types; conflating them hides the mechanism.

Core concepts

Three independent axes

Watch each mechanism spend compute differently. Use the controls on small screens; on larger screens the diagrams animate as they enter view.

Axis 01

Parallel sampling (best-of-n)

Draw many independent solutions at once, then pick the best with a reward model, verifier, or exact checker (unit tests, proof assistant). Cost grows with sample count; realized accuracy is coverage filtered by selector quality.

Accuracy driver
Coverage × verifier precision
Helps most
Code, proofs, math with strong checkers
Failure mode
False positives accumulate as n grows

Axis 02

Tree / graph search

Expand intermediate reasoning steps as a tree (or graph), score partial states, and allocate budget to promising branches — Tree-of-Thoughts, MCTS-guided decoding, step-level beam search. Structure matters; sample count alone does not.

Accuracy driver
Value estimates for prune & expand
Helps most
Planning, multi-step math, agent tasks
Failure mode
Weak value model wastes FLOPs vs sampling

Axis 03

Self-consistency (majority voting)

Sample many chain-of-thought paths and return the most common final answer — no external scorer and no search tree. Gains come from answer concentration; the method saturates once the modal answer is fixed.

Accuracy driver
Concentration on the correct mode
Helps most
Arithmetic, multiple choice, short answers
Failure mode
Saturates; can amplify a wrong majority

Cross-axis view

Allocate compute along each dimension

Most recent high-performing methods sit in the interior as hybrids. The paper’s original contribution is a gap analysis and a factorial study design that would finally measure each axis in isolation.

What the paper asks next

  1. A shared cost metric (tokens, FLOPs, latency, dollars).
  2. Ablations that vary one axis while holding others fixed.
  3. Verifier quality isolated from the search or sampling method.
  4. A task taxonomy that predicts which axis scales best.
